# Data Management

## Course Overview

Data Management builds proficiency in working with both relational and non-relational databases. Learners gain hands-on experience in selecting appropriate storage architectures, understanding data types and structures, and applying principles of normalization and denormalization.

The course covers SQL (Structured Query Language) fundamentals, including Data Definition Language (DDL) and Data Manipulation Language (DML), with practical exercises in joins, aggregations, and transactions. Learners also contrast relational approaches with non-relational methods to understand when and how to adapt data storage architectures to fit diverse business needs.

This foundation enables learners to design efficient data models, implement them in real systems, and query databases to support business decision-making.
## Key Learnings

Through this course and its programming tasks, I gained experience in:

- Data Architecture Design
Recommended appropriate data storage solutions (relational vs. non-relational) based on business requirements.

- Data Exploration & Quality Assessment
Examined available data to evaluate its dimensions, quality, relationships, and limitations before analysis.

- Logical Data Modeling
Constructed logical data models to represent entities, attributes, and relationships.

- Physical Data Modeling
Implemented physical database models by defining tables, keys, and constraints in SQL.

- Querying for Business Insights
Performed SQL queries, including joins, aggregations, and filtering, to answer real business questions.

- Data Manipulation & Management
Applied DML statements (INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE) and transactions to manage data consistently.
